Question 4 of 7
Is your data hall air-cooled or liquid-cooled?

This decides what the next question even means. A liquid hall has a CDU between your facility loop and the chips, and the temperature that matters is the one the cold plates are promised. An air hall has chilled water going straight to coils, and the temperature that matters is what leaves the plant. Pick one and we will draw your loop.

Question 5 of 7
What water do your racks actually get?

Your IT does not see facility water — it sees whatever comes out of the CDU, one heat exchanger later. So we ask for the number your racks are promised and the step the CDU adds — the SLA ceiling comes on the next page, where IceBrick makes it usable. Facility water is then whatever the plant has to make to deliver it, and that is the loop the entire before analysis runs on.

On the 2nd–5th day of a heat wave the condensers stop seeing the weather station's ambient — their own discharge recirculates over the intakes. Estimate the degrees that adds. It is applied only in hours above 90 °F (32.2 °C), and only to the kW/ton of the chillers IceBrick avoids: once the Storage cuts more than ~30% of the plant's load, the recirculation cell collapses and the surviving machines get the real ambient back. Like to go bigger on your storage size?

One night is the natural unit: bank at dusk what tomorrow will spend, and the IceBrick array never carries anything past breakfast. Build it deeper and it can do something a single night cannot — bank on a mild night ahead of a hot run, and spend it on the days in that run whose own nights are too warm to charge properly. That is worth real peak, but only up to a point, and the curve below is where the point is.

Side by side

Your year, before and after

Same axes, same scale. The red line is what the grid sees. Watch the spikes disappear — and notice that the flat line sits barely above the old baseline, because the energy was moved, not added. Blue is that move: load taken off the day and put back at night. Pink is what the move buys on top of itself — the surviving chillers running at lower lift because the Storage is post-cooling them, and the whole plant sliding down its part-load curve because it is carrying less. There is no pink at night: the ice is frozen by a designated set of chillers on their own separate, colder circuit, so the machines holding the facility never carry the charging duty and never feel it on their part-load curve. The night band is pure blue — the ice circuit's own electric, and nothing else. Drag to zoom either chart — the other follows, so you are always comparing the same hours.
